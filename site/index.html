Step 1 of 8 · Chemical space

Every sphere is one drug

Each point is a drug from the curated DrugBank-derived table (one point per PubChem compound, salts removed). Its position comes from a principal component analysis of nine RDKit descriptors computed from the curated SMILES (sizes and counts on a log scale, so a few large peptides do not flatten the rest), so drugs with similar size, polarity and lipophilicity sit close together.

Drag to turn the space, pinch or Ctrl + scroll to zoom, and hover or tap a sphere to name it.

Step 2 of 8 · Structure

A map of shared scaffolds

The same drugs, now placed by structure rather than by properties. Each drug's Morgan count fingerprint (radius 3, 2048 bits, from marimo-chem-utils) is reduced to two dimensions with t-SNE, so drugs built on the same scaffold land next to each other. Distances between distant clusters mean little in t-SNE; only the neighbourhoods do.

A Hansch equation compares drugs that share a scaffold and differ by substituents. Neighbours on this map are the closest thing this set has to such a series.

Step 3 of 8 · Hydrophobicity

log P, the π term

Hansch and Fujita (1964) wrote biological activity as a function of hydrophobicity, electronic effects and size: log 1/C = −k π² + k′ π + ρ σ + k″. A drug has to partition into membranes to reach its target, so potency often rises with log P and then falls again once the molecule is too greasy to leave the membrane.

The points now spread along one axis only: Crippen log P, computed by RDKit the same way for every drug. PubChem's XLogP3 is shown beside it in the drug panel and as an axis in Explore.

Step 4 of 8 · Size

Molar refractivity, the steric term

Molar refractivity (MR, cm³/mol) tracks molecular volume and polarisability. In Hansch equations it stands in for the bulk that has to fit the binding site. Seen from above, how log P and MR relate depends on size, which is why a Hansch equation keeps them as separate terms.

Step 5 of 8 · Polarity

Polar surface area and the oral route

Topological polar surface area (TPSA, Å²) adds the third axis. It is a whole-molecule stand-in for hydrogen bonding, not a Hammett σ. Veber et al. (2002) found that oral absorption drops above 140 Å², drawn here as a plane. Blue spheres are drugs with an oral route in the curated table.

Step 6 of 8 · Activity

Potency at the mechanism target

The vertical axis now shows activity: the median ChEMBL pChEMBL value (−log₁₀ molar IC50, Ki, EC50 or Kd) at the drug's mechanism target, kept only when at least two publications agree. Drugs without a value leave the scene.

Step 7 of 8 · One target

A series that shares a target

The surface is the fitted equation. Each thin line joins a drug to the surface, so its length is the residual.

Step 8 of 8 · Many targets

Mixing targets breaks the model

Hansch analysis assumes one mechanism and a congeneric series. Across unrelated targets, potency depends on the binding site far more than on bulk physical properties.

    Method and checks

    • Drugs. One point per PubChem compound in the curated table drugbank_curated_dedup.csv, which is the input of the data build (the curation step that produced it is not part of this repository). The page does not verify the regulatory status of any drug. The curated SMILES is the neutral parent; its InChIKey finds the parent CID in PubChem.
    • Descriptors. The main explorer uses RDKit on the curated SMILES: Crippen log P and molar refractivity, TPSA (Ertl, N and O), molecular weight, N/O-bound hydrogens (CalcNumLipinskiHBD), N + O atoms (CalcNumLipinskiHBA), rotatable bonds, aromatic rings and Fsp3. These Lipinski counts differ from the frozen DRD2 QSAR’s donor/acceptor sites (NumHDonors → CalcNumHBD; NumHAcceptors → CalcNumHBA). The main explorer’s PCA and regressions use its curated descriptor table; the DRD2 overlay uses its separate frozen export. PubChem PUG REST adds XLogP3 and complexity where it has the parent record, and its weight and TPSA are a cross-check.
    • Structure map. marimo-chem-utils: Morgan count fingerprints (radius 3, 2048 bits), PCA to 50 components, then t-SNE (seed 42). The same library checks that each curated SMILES gives back its curated InChIKey. tools/hansch_marimo.py is a marimo notebook over the same data.
    • 3D conformers. PubChem's first 3D conformer where one exists; otherwise RDKit ETKDGv3 with an MMFF clean-up, labelled as such.
    • Activity. Median ChEMBL pChEMBL at the mechanism target, needing two or more documents. Toxicity is still empty in the source table.
    • Regressions. Ordinary least squares on log P, log P², MR and TPSA, for drugs up to 1000 Da (Crippen log P of large peptides reaches −43 and would set every slope). Model choice by leave-one-out q²; chance correlation checked by refitting on 100 shuffled activity vectors.
    • Headset and phone AR. The Enter VR and View in AR buttons appear only when your browser reports WebXR support for that mode; without it they stay hidden and everything else works the same. The WebXR code has been exercised only on an emulated runtime, not on a physical headset or phone.

    Rebuild the data with python tools/build_data.py. Every PubChem and WHO request it makes is listed in data/provenance.csv, and the cross-checks are in data/validation.md. The sources, their terms and the changes made to them are listed under Data sources and licences.

      How the workflow works · from assay records to a checked model
      1. Collect one endpoint. ChEMBL records describe the single human DRD2 protein, Ki and binding assays (B). Keep exact measurements (=), nM source units, a reported pChEMBL value, assay confidence ≥8, no data-validity comment and no potential-duplicate flag. This is a human target assignment; confidence 8 can include homologous or unspecified-source assays. Only pChEMBL enters the model; −log₁₀ of molar concentration means higher values indicate stronger measured binding.
      2. Resolve compound identity. RDKit parses structures, strips salts, neutralizes charges, removes solvents, keeps the largest fragment and chooses a canonical tautomer before generating a full InChIKey. Each retained compound needs at least two independent document IDs and a replicate spread ≤1 log unit; its response is the median pChEMBL. Molecules above 1000 Da are excluded.
      3. Calculate descriptors and fit. RDKit supplies lipophilicity, refractivity, polarity, size, hydrogen-bond counts and other molecular descriptors, including squared log P. Zero-variance terms are removed; pairs with |Pearson r| >0.9 keep the term more correlated with activity. Ordinary least squares uses at most one descriptor per five compounds. Every training fold repeats descriptor selection using its own training responses.
      4. Validate beyond the fit. A fixed-seed Murcko scaffold split aims for 80/20 while keeping scaffolds together. Five-fold scaffold CV, leave-one-out q² and 100 complete y-scrambled refits test generalization and chance correlation. The Williams plot flags leverage above 3p/n or standardized residuals beyond ±3. Coefficient standard errors, p-values and VIF describe uncertainty and collinearity; post-selection p-values remain descriptive.
      5. Read the conclusion, then inspect records. Sharing a target does not make these compounds a congeneric series. The training equation below explains the stored plot values; the separate full-data fit is descriptive.

      Changes made to the source data

      Generic names have counter-ions removed (14 names). Structures are PubChem SMILES, neutralised and re-canonicalised with RDKit, one record per PubChem compound. Potency is the median of all ChEMBL pChEMBL values at the drug's mechanism targets, kept only when at least two documents and two assays report one. WHO route codes were mapped to FDA route names (the WHO code P becomes PARENTERAL, which is coarse). Drugs are grouped by their first ChEMBL mechanism target for the fits. Every PubChem and WHO request the data build made is listed in data/provenance.csv; cross-checks are in data/validation.md. The requests made in the earlier curation step (ChEMBL, openFDA, DailyMed) are not listed in this repository.

      About the data

      Drug Hansch Space is an educational exploration of 759 drugs. It is not medical advice, does not recommend, rank, dose or diagnose, and is not a toxicity, safety or efficacy predictor. Toxicity is not part of the data. The 3D positions come from descriptors computed with RDKit from each drug's structure, not from experiments on that drug. The Hansch-type fits relate the median ChEMBL pChEMBL potency to log P, molar refractivity and TPSA within groups of drugs that share a mechanism target. Those groups are not congeneric series, the potencies are medians across different assays, and TPSA is a whole-molecule polarity proxy, so the fits are exploratory; the page labels each one "predictive within this set", "weak" or "not predictive", and most are weak or not predictive. No model on this page predicts how a drug behaves in a person. ATC groups classify drugs for drug-utilisation statistics; membership of a group does not mean therapeutic equivalence or a difference in effectiveness. Routes of administration are summaries of WHO codes and US label listings, not dosing or labelling information, and the DailyMed "in use" labelling may differ from the FDA's own labelling. Source records can be incomplete, outdated or wrong. The site is provided "as is" without warranty and is not affiliated with or endorsed by DrugBank, EMBL-EBI, WHO or its Collaborating Centre, NCBI/NLM, the FDA or any other source named.

      Therapeutic index

      Loading the ranked batch…

      Not a clinical therapeutic index. This is an in vitro selectivity margin over one liability (the hERG cardiac channel), for putting a batch of molecules in order. It is not LD50/ED50, it says nothing about dose, and it is not evidence that anything here is safe in a person.

      Sorted by the margin, widest first. Each whole number is a tenfold difference: an index of 2.0 means the molecule grips its intended target about a hundred times more tightly than it grips the heart channel. Rows marked outside fall beyond the model's applicability domain and should be read as suggestions at best.

      Rank in targetMoleculeTarget ActivityhERGIndex Domain

      A molecule's activity may only come from a model fitted on its own intended target, with the same assay type and the same organism. There is no falling back to a related protein and none to a general model across targets. A number from the wrong target's model is worse than no number, because on the page it looks exactly like a right one. So where no model applies, the cell is empty and the row says why.
